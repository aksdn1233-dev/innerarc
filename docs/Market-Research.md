# Market Research

Last reviewed: 2026-07-26  
Status: Product-pattern desk research from public first-party pages. It is not a market-size, legal, trademark, pricing, or user-interview substitute.

## Method and limits

- Product capabilities below are evidence from official product or store pages rechecked on 2026-07-26.
- “Opportunity/weakness” statements are InnerArc product inferences from what those public pages do or do not describe. They are not claims about undisclosed product behavior or user satisfaction.
- Prices, feature limits, rankings, and policies change. Recheck them immediately before a commercial decision.
- Astrology products are included because they compete for the same self-discovery, relationship, daily-guidance, and symbolic-reflection jobs even though InnerArc does not implement natal astrology in the MVP.

## System synthesis for InnerArc

### Build into the core

1. A useful free result before payment.
2. Transparent, deterministic numerology facts and auditable tarot provenance.
3. Layered disclosure: short first answer, optional method and deeper report.
4. Private relationship comparison with explicit third-party-data limits.
5. Manual tarot entry, export, deletion, and long-term reflection.
6. Reality Check as the primary retention loop: interpretation → choice → action → outcome → relevance → pattern report.
7. Native Korean and English meaning parity rather than literal translation.
8. Calm lifestyle curation that turns a result into optional, testable style and sound directions.
9. Searchable, category-filtered history after authenticated persistence exists, with outcome review more prominent than repeating the same reading.
10. Beginner-first copy with expert detail behind disclosure, especially for Korean terminology and calculation rules.

### Improve on common weaknesses

1. Replace accuracy and fate language with symbolic hypothesis, uncertainty, and decision ownership.
2. Separate calculated facts, traditional symbolism, personalized inference, commercial placement, and real outcome.
3. Let users record “not relevant” and show misses in reports rather than optimizing only for confirming feedback.
4. Keep safety help, data export, and deletion outside paywalls.
5. Do not infer consent to store another person’s birth data from a compatibility request.
6. Avoid notification fear, streak guilt, soulmate certainty, lucky-object claims, and opaque AI-selected cards.
7. Keep the first screen focused despite broad feature depth.
8. Do not let a virtual-credit balance, advertising, product placement, or a “try again” loop displace a pending outcome review.
9. Keep third-party profiles private and purpose-limited; sharing a symbolic result never implies permission to expose stored birth data.

## Market evidence already translated into product behavior

| Market strength or recurring weakness | InnerArc implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Rich journals make prior readings easy to revisit, but often stop at the reading itself. | Reality Check stores the question, choice, action, review date, outcome, relevance, and learning; a relationship context can now become an editable one-time draft directly. |
| Broad catalogs improve discovery but create choice overload. | The first viewport emphasizes one action; deeper profile, evidence, lifestyle, relationship, and shop-preview sections use progressive disclosure or separate routes. |
| Daily content and repeat-reading buttons can support habit but also encourage compulsive checking. | No guilt streak, fear notification, or unrestricted “draw again until satisfied” loop is positioned as the retention model; outcome return is the primary loop. |
| Scores, forecasts, “lucky” objects, and compatibility percentages are commercially legible but overstate symbolic evidence. | Results use hypotheses, conditions, relevance, and observable checks. Accessory/music cards reject efficacy claims, and the shop cannot open through recommendation logic. |
| Profile groups and social comparison add value while increasing third-party-data exposure. | Compatibility is private and session-only in the guest web MVP, share payloads exclude raw identifiers, and future invitations require authenticated owner controls. |
| Expert systems earn trust through visible depth. | Numerology facts and tarot draw provenance are code-generated and versioned; Korean/English meaning parity and expandable evidence remain release gates. |

## Lifestyle and commerce hypothesis

Result-based accessories and music are adjacent-value experiments, not proven core demand:

- Accessories can make an abstract profile tangible through form, palette, material, and use context. The risk is turning symbolism into pseudoscientific product claims or exploitative upsell.
- Music direction can make reflection immediately experiential without collecting streaming history. The risk is stereotyped genre assignment, cultural flattening, or mental-health claims.
- The MVP therefore recommends categories and listening directions only, includes a reality check, and keeps the shop closed.
- A shop should open only if users voluntarily revisit or save these suggestions and trust/safety interviews show that recommendations feel useful rather than manipulative.

## Validation plan

1. Run at least five moderated tests each with numerology/tarot users and journaling/self-growth users.
2. Measure first-result comprehension, trust, save intent, and willingness to schedule a Reality Check.
3. Test whether accessory/music cards improve result usefulness without lowering trust or making the product feel like an upsell.
4. Require users to distinguish “symbolic style suggestion” from luck, healing, or prediction in comprehension testing.
5. Track category-open intent only with consented aggregate events; collect no raw result or concern.
6. Validate Korea/US pricing with paywall-choice experiments and actual conversion, not stated willingness alone.
7. Recheck competitor features, regional stores, privacy practices, prices, and brand availability immediately before launch.
